The short answer

Strong core and connected data, but missing the outside connections buyers expect

Where get-ly is already ahead

Every product writes to one shared set of books. A booking becomes a contact, a meeting and an invoice, and payroll and stock post real ledger entries. Standalone tools need paid connectors to do this, and they often go out of sync.

Where it matches

The core of each app: ledger and reports, pipeline, stock and counts, leave and payroll maths, booking pages, chat inbox and tickets, a post planner, and webhooks.

Where it falls behind

Connections to the outside world: sending email, taking payments, bank feeds, WhatsApp, calendar sync, direct social posting, AI and mobile. These are the reasons a buyer would say no today.

The real competitor

The real competition is the other all-in-one suites, Odoo and Zoho One, more than any single app. Both already sell dozens of apps cheaply, so being all-in-one won't win buyers by itself. The plan at the end is about where to be different.

Strategy

How to disrupt the market

Get-ly won't win by having more features than Odoo and Zoho. They have years of head start and hundreds of developers. It can win by picking a fight they can't easily fight back. These six moves build on what get-ly already has.

1. Sell an AI back office that does the work, with the software included

Rivals add AI to each app separately. Get-ly has every product in one database, so one assistant can do jobs that span apps:

  • match the bank statement against invoices;
  • chase overdue invoices;
  • draft chat replies from past answers;
  • flag stock that will run out before the next order;
  • explain why profit fell this month, using sales, wages and stock costs together.

The message to buyers: "your bookkeeper, receptionist and analyst, for the price of one app." Have it suggest each action and wait for a click to approve, so trust is built step by step.

2. Price per company, never per seat

Most rivals charge per user, per app. Charge one flat price per company with every app included and unlimited staff, and keep a real free plan. That makes adding a fifth employee or a second app free, which is exactly where rivals' bills jump. The prices themselves are your call.

3. Win one type of customer completely before going wide

Service businesses with 5 to 50 people already use almost every get-ly app in a chain: booking → meeting → invoice → payment → payroll. Examples are clinics, salons, agencies, consultancies, tutors and repair shops. Build a ready-made setup for one of them, with its services, invoice template, chat answers and reports. "Get-ly for clinics" is easier to sell than "an all-in-one suite".

4. Go deep on a local market the giants serve badly

Global tools treat many regions as an afterthought. Pick your home market and own it:

  • local tax rules on invoices;
  • local payroll deductions;
  • local payment methods and instant bank transfers;
  • local languages;
  • WhatsApp first.

In Pakistan, for example, that means FBR sales tax, EOBI and provincial social security, and Raast and mobile wallets. Local depth like this is what makes Zoho or Odoo look generic.

5. Use accountants as your sales team

The group and consolidation features already let one login manage many companies. Turn that into a free accountant view: every client's books, payroll and deadlines in one screen. One accountant can bring 20 to 50 small businesses, which is how Xero grew.

6. Make switching take one afternoon

Offer importers for the common rivals, a free "we move your data" service for the first customers, and a side-by-side check that the old and new trial balances match. Fear of losing data is why people stay with tools they don't like.
